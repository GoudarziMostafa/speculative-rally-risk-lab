# Model notes

- **Price path.** The underlying starts at 1 at entry. Its ordinary daily multiplier is `exp(log(1 + m) - s²/2 + sZ)`, where `m` is the expected simple return, `s` is daily volatility and `Z` is a standard normal draw. While active, the expected gain holds for the preset's chosen duration and then fades linearly to zero. The model uses the next day's age after each step.
- **Breaks.** On active day `a`, the break probability is the initial probability plus `a` times the daily increase, capped at 100%. On the break day, the price multiplier also includes a loss drawn uniformly from the selected range. Subsequent days use an illustrative expected simple return of −2% and the same volatility. The probability of reaching entry day `d` without a break is the product of the no-break probabilities for ages 0 through `d − 1`. If that probability is zero, the entry simulation still describes a hypothetical active rally.
- **Execution.** Stock take-profit and trailing-stop rules trigger at observed daily prices. A daily move of at least 25% can apply an adverse exit price penalty with the selected probability. The round-trip fee rate is split equally across entry notional and exit proceeds. Cash returns zero.
- **Margin.** Interest accrues against the original loan at the selected annual simple rate over 252 trading days. Margin liquidates at the first observed price at or below the maintenance equity ratio, subject to the selected execution costs. A gap can create a loss greater than 100% of committed equity.
- **Calls.** Entry and early exit use a Black–Scholes benchmark with zero rates and dividends. Entry implied volatility defaults to daily stock volatility times the square root of 252. A manual value can be shared across all scenarios. The post-break multiplier defaults to 100% of entry implied volatility and affects early-exit marks on or after the break. The call fee charges half the selected rate on the entry premium and half on early-sale proceeds. Early exits follow the stock-based exit rules. At expiration, the call settles at intrinsic value without an exit fee. A zero modeled entry premium leaves percentage returns undefined.
- **Comparison and reproducibility.** The lab uses 4,000 paths per scenario with seed 42. Each path has a fixed set of draws for ordinary moves, break timing, severity and execution. Comparisons reuse those draws at matching days since entry, so changes in exits do not shift the draws available to later paths. Scenario weights apply to individual outcomes before percentiles are calculated. Portfolio impact multiplies the trade return by the share of total portfolio equity committed to the trade, with the remainder in cash. Loss chances are displayed as whole percentages. At 4,000 paths, the Monte Carlo standard error for one scenario's loss chance is at most about 0.8 percentage points.

The simulation uses daily observations and illustrative parameters. Real-stock inputs such as short interest, float, borrow fees and announced offerings can inform which assumptions to stress-test. The parameters represent user-selected conditions rather than estimates from market data.

## Research

- Barber, Huang, Odean, and Schwarz found a −4.7% average 20-day abnormal return for the top stocks purchased each day by Robinhood users. This motivates testing fading buying pressure. [*Attention-Induced Trading and Returns*, Journal of Finance](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13183)
- Zaynutdinova, Strong, and Baig report continued gains in a sample of potential gamma squeezes selected for persistent hedging pressure. Their working paper uses the next 22 trading days in its event definition. That motivates testing persistent pressure as a scenario and using information available on the entry date in any future historical evaluation. [*Seeking Gamma: Lessons from the Meme Frenzy*](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5959235)
